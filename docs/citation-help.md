# Citation Help

## What it's for

AI can help you format citations (MLA, APA, Chicago) and build a bibliography
entry from a source you already have. It's a helper, not a citation machine —
always double-check the result against your school's style guide.

## Step by step

1. Gather the source details yourself: author, title, publication, date, URL or
   page numbers.
2. Paste the details into a free AI chat.
3. Ask for the citation in your required style: "Cite this in MLA 9th edition."
4. Ask for the in-text citation too: "What does the in-text citation look like?"
5. Copy the result into your bibliography.
6. Double-check every citation against an official style guide (Purdue OWL,
   your library's citation page, or your style manual).

## Example prompts you can copy

```
Cite this source in MLA 9th edition: [paste source details]. Also give me the
in-text citation format.
```

```
Here are 3 sources. Format them as an APA 7th edition references list: [paste
details].
```

```
Is this Chicago-style footnote formatted correctly? [paste footnote]
```

## Privacy check

- Do NOT paste grades or your student ID number.
- Citation details about a published source are generally fine to paste. Just
  don't include unpublished work that belongs to you or someone else without
  permission.

## Important

AI sometimes invents page numbers, authors, or publication dates (this is
called hallucinating). Never trust an AI citation blindly — verify every
author, title, date, and page number yourself.

## Free citation resources

- Purdue OWL (mla, apa, chicago guides) — free online
- Your college library's citation help page
- Zotero — free citation management software you can install
