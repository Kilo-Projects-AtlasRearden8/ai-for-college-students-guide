Email Drafts

Draft emails to professors, academic advisers, financial aid, or campus offices. AI can help you strike a polite, clear tone — especially when you’re not sure how to start.

Step by step

  1. Decide what you need to say and what you want the recipient to do.
  2. Paste the key facts into a free AI chat: who you’re emailing, why, and what you’re asking for.
  3. Ask for a draft: “Write a short, polite email to my professor asking for an extension on the [assignment] due [date].”
  4. Read the draft out loud. Does it sound like you? Change anything that doesn’t.
  5. Fill in your name, class name, and section number.
  6. Double-check the recipient’s email address before sending.

Example prompts you can copy

Write a short, polite email to my professor asking for an extension on the
[essay name] due [date]. I'm asking for [number] extra days. Keep it under 4
short paragraphs.
Write an email to my academic adviser asking how to drop a class without it
affecting my financial aid. Include my class name and section number as
placeholders.
Write a polite follow-up email to the financial aid office asking about the
status of my application. I submitted it on [date].

Privacy check: Do not paste your student ID number in the email draft. Do not paste grades. You can include placeholders like [student ID] and fill them in yourself outside the AI chat. Don’t paste classmates’ names or personal information.

Remember: Read every AI draft out loud before sending. If it sounds stiff or wrong, rewrite it in your own words. You’re responsible for what you send.
